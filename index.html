Rumack-Matthew Nomogram Plotter

How to use: Enter the exact time that has elapsed since the acute ingestion (for staggered ingestions, since the first ingestion), along with the measured acetaminophen (APAP) level, then click "Plot Level". The nomogram is only valid for blood samples taken between 4 and 24 hours post-ingestion. Use the "Hours" and "Minutes" fields together to specify the time of the lab draw (e.g., for a draw at 4.5 hours, enter 4 in Hours and 30 in Minutes).

Use the nomogram when

All of the following should generally be true:

  • The patient had a single, acute ingestion, usually of an immediate-release product. (Staggered ingestions may also qualify — see Special situations.)
  • The time of ingestion is known and reliable.
  • The APAP level was drawn 4–24 hours after ingestion.
  • The patient does not already show signs of liver injury from acetaminophen.

Do not use the nomogram for

  • Intravenous (IV) acetaminophen overdose. The nomogram has not been validated for IV exposures.

Special situations (2023 US/Canadian consensus)

  • Staggered ingestions within 24 hours: Can be used if the ingestion pattern and start time are reliable. Plot the time from the first ingestion.
  • Extended-release APAP: Can be used. If a level drawn 4–12 hours after ingestion is below the treatment line (150 line) but above 10 µg/mL, repeat the level in 4–6 hours.
  • Opioid or anticholinergic co-ingestion: Can be used. If the level is below the treatment line but above 10 µg/mL and the patient shows opioid or anticholinergic effects, repeat the level in 4–6 hours.
  • Pregnancy: Not a contraindication. Use the same nomogram and treatment threshold.

Source: Dart RC, et al. Management of Acetaminophen Poisoning in the US and Canada: A Consensus Statement. JAMA Netw Open. 2023;6(8):e2327739.
